# Detection Report: Unidentified Compact Source in the Target System

**Target:** planetary system TS-1, range 4.1 light-years
**Observation window:** 41 days, continuous
**Instruments:** infrared sensor IR-1, astrometric tracker AS-2, Doppler receiver DR-3, radio receiver RR-4, photometer PH-5

## Summary

Five instruments registered a single compact source inside the target system. The source radiates heat with no nearby star to warm it. The source changes velocity with no gravitational cause. The source emits a radio line far narrower than any natural width. The source flashes at a fixed period. No object in the onboard catalogue, natural or artificial, matches the combined record.

## Thermal

Sensor IR-1 measured a blackbody spectrum that peaks near 10 micrometers and fits a temperature of 291 kelvin. At the source distance from the system star, starlight alone holds a passive body near 40 kelvin. The source therefore supplies its own heat. The radiated power reaches 600 megawatts. The source stayed unresolved, which bounds its width below 3 kilometers.

## Motion

Tracker AS-2 and receiver DR-3 fixed the source on a bound path inside the target system. Across the window the path departed from the gravitational solution by an amount equal to a sustained acceleration of 0.028 meters per second squared. The direction of that acceleration changed twice. Cometary outgassing at this distance produces accelerations more than a thousand times smaller.

## Emission

Receiver RR-4 detected one emission line near 203 gigahertz. The line width stayed below 0.2 hertz, a fractional width near one part in a trillion. Natural sources hold lines no narrower than one part in a million. The line center drifted at the same rate as the measured acceleration of the source.

## Photometry

Photometer PH-5 recorded brightness spikes that repeat every 3.9 seconds. Each spike rises fifty times above the baseline and lasts below 40 milliseconds. The spike profile matches reflection from flat surfaces, not scattering from a rough body.

## Cross-check

The five instruments operate on independent detection chains and agree on the source position to within their stated tolerances. The onboard catalogue holds no natural body at these coordinates and no artificial transmitter at this frequency, drift, or trajectory.

## Candidate explanations

Three candidates remain. An engineered object under propulsion accounts for the heat, the acceleration, the narrow line, and the flat-surface flashes together. A natural body accounts for the heat and part of the acceleration, and fails against the line width and the specular flashes. An instrument fault accounts for one channel, and fails against the agreement of five independent chains. The first candidate matches every measurement. The remaining candidates stay open above zero.

## Reproduce

Point sensor IR-1 and receiver RR-4 at the listed coordinates. Sample for one full flash period at millisecond cadence. Fold the photometer record at 3.9 seconds. Compare the line drift rate against the trajectory residual.
